## 1. Background

The credit-conditions index (CCI) is compiled from the monthly lender survey. At each month-end the survey's panel
of lenders says whether their lending standards eased, held or tightened over the month, and the index is the
balance of those answers, scaled so that 50 means no change: above 50, conditions eased on balance; below 50, they
tightened.

Easier credit has tended to come before stronger equity markets, and the index has been on the desk's screens for
years. The evidence so far is exploratory: several versions of the idea were tried on overlapping data, and the
best of them was reported. This study tests one version, fixed in this document before the analysis is run. If
the development sample supports it, a confirmation study on later data follows (section 9).

## 2. Hypothesis

- **H1.** A rise in the CCI over a month is followed by a higher return on the equity index in the next month.
- **H0.** The next month's return does not depend on the month's movement in the CCI.

An effect also has to be large enough to act on. After trading costs, and given how noisy the index is from month
to month, the team agreed that a slope below 0.20 percentage points of monthly return per index point is too small
to use in allocation, whatever its p-value. That is the study's minimum effect (section 6).

## 3. Data

### 3.1 The development sample

The development sample is two files in `data/dev/` (the study folder's `README.md` says what is where), one row
per month from January 2004 to December 2023:

- `signal.csv`: `month` (`YYYY-MM`) and `cci`, the index as published at that month's end;
- `returns.csv`: `month` (`YYYY-MM`) and `return_pct`, the equity index's total return over that calendar month,
  in percent (`1.25` is a return of 1.25%).

The files are used as delivered: they are read, never edited, and every step below is done in the analysis code.

### 3.2 The confirmation sample

The months from January 2024 to June 2026 are the confirmation sample, kept for the confirmation study (section 9).

## 4. Analysis

The analysis takes the six steps below in this order, each working on what the step before it leaves. Together
they fix every choice that moves the numbers, so the result does not depend on the software used.

**Step 1: timing.** The index for a month is published at that month's end, so it can only bear on later returns.
Each month's index is paired with the return of the month that follows it: the CCI for March 2010 with the return
for April 2010. A month whose following return is not in the development sample has no pair and is not used.

**Step 2: the signal.** The signal is the month's movement in the index: that month's CCI less the CCI of the
month before, in index points. It is worked out on the full monthly series in `signal.csv`, before any month is
excluded in step 4. The first month of the sample has no month before it, so it has no signal and is not used.

**Step 3: units.** Returns are used in percent, as `returns.csv` records them, and are not converted to decimals.
The slope is therefore in percentage points of return per index point, the unit of the minimum effect.

**Step 4: excluded months.** Eight months are excluded, each for a reason to do with the return itself. A pair is
dropped when its return falls in one of these months: the exclusion goes by the month of the return, not the month
of the index.

| Return month | Reason |
|---|---|
| 2005-06 | The index provider moved from price weights to free-float weights during the month; the month's return mixes the two methods. |
| 2008-10 | The exchange closed for three trading days; the return covers a shortened month. |
| 2010-05 | A failed opening auction was cancelled after the fact, and the provider restated the month's return twice. |
| 2011-08 | Short selling in the index's bank stocks was banned for most of the month. |
| 2013-06 | The index was rebalanced twice in the month after an error in a corporate action. |
| 2016-06 | The settlement system failed on the last two days of the month, and closing prices came from the fallback auction. |
| 2020-03 | Trading was halted by circuit breakers four times; several closing prices came from the fallback auction. |
| 2022-09 | The index changed its base currency; the month's return includes the conversion. |

**Step 5: extreme returns.** After the exclusions, returns are capped at the 1st and 99th percentiles of the
returns in the pairs that are left: a return below the 1st percentile is set to the 1st percentile, and one above
the 99th is set to the 99th. The signal is not capped. Percentiles are worked out by linear interpolation between
ranks: with the n returns sorted from lowest to highest, the p-th percentile sits at rank 1 + (n - 1) x p / 100,
and a rank between two whole numbers takes the value on the straight line between the two returns either side of
it (the default method in R, numpy and pandas).

**Step 6: the test.** An ordinary least-squares regression of the capped return on the signal, with an intercept,
over the pairs left after step 4. Reported are the slope and the intercept; the slope's t statistic, the slope
divided by its usual OLS standard error; the p-value of that t statistic, two-sided, from Student's t distribution
with n - 2 degrees of freedom, where n is the number of pairs in the regression; R-squared (unadjusted); and
Pearson's correlation r between the two columns as they enter the regression (the signal, and the capped return).

The p-value is two-sided although H1 names a direction. The committee wants to hear of an effect in either
direction, and a one-sided test chosen because of the direction we expect is the kind of choice this study is
meant to rule out.

## 5. Sample size

The development sample has twenty years of months. The exploratory work suggests that is enough to detect an
effect of the minimum size, if there is one.

## 6. Decision

The hypothesis is supported if, and only if, both of these hold:

1. the p-value of step 6 is below 0.05; and
2. the slope is at least the minimum effect, 0.20 percentage points of next-month return per index point.

Otherwise it is not supported. A verdict of not supported is a complete result and is reported as found.

## 7. Reporting

The result goes to the research committee on the template in `results/RESULTS.md`: the numbers of step 6, the
number of pairs used, and the decision in three fields: `significant` (yes when the p-value is below 0.05),
`meets_minimum_effect` (yes when the slope is at least the minimum effect) and `verdict`.

Further analysis, such as another specification or a robustness check, may be reported in the template's comments,
marked as exploratory. It never replaces a registered number or the verdict.

## 8. Deviations

Any change to this plan, whatever its reason, is reported as a deviation: what was done instead, and why. The
registered analysis is still reported, and a deviation does not change the verdict, which is always the registered
analysis's.

## 9. Data handling

The confirmation sample in `/sealed/confirmation/` is reserved for the confirmation study and is not to be opened,
copied or searched by any tool before it. The confirmation study will have its own registration, which will say
when the sample is opened and by whom.
