Title: Weekly wrap: 26 to 30 October
Date: 2026-10-30
Desk: Strategy

The week

The quality factor has had its best quarter in two years, helped by falling bond volatility. We stay
neutral on equities against bonds, with a preference for quality and cash flow. We prefer to express
a view on growth through credit rather than through cyclical equities. Our main risk scenario is a
sharper slowdown in the region's trading partners.

The Valdorian economy grew 0.4% quarter on quarter in the third quarter in the first estimate (30
October), below our 0.5% forecast.

The data have been patchy rather than weak, and the month brought few surprises. Government bond
issuance plans for next year will be published with the budget, and we expect few surprises.
Investment intentions are strongest in energy and logistics, and weakest in construction. Housing
activity has bottomed, but a strong rebound is unlikely while mortgage rates stay where they are.
Firms report that hiring intentions have cooled, although layoffs remain rare. Survey evidence
points to a uneven recovery in services, while manufacturing remains the weak spot. Consumer credit
growth has picked up, mainly in card spending.

Markets

Valdoria's 10-year yield closed at 3.39% on Thursday, near the top of its range for the month.

The dollar index slipped 2.1% over the month. The euro traded between 1.63 and 1.81 against the
dollar. One-month implied volatility in the major currency pairs averaged 9.4%.

The market is pricing a gentler path of cuts than the central banks' own projections imply. We would
fade any move that relies on one data release alone. Insurers have been buyers of inflation-linked
bonds on any back-up in yields. Inflation-linked bonds look fairly priced against our inflation
outlook. Central bank communication has shifted from guidance to data dependence, which adds to
volatility. We prefer to hold steepeners in the five-to-thirty-year sector.

Bank loan markets have reopened for mid-sized companies, which takes pressure off bond issuance.
Insurers have been net buyers of investment-grade credit for most of the quarter so far. Ten-year
paper offers little extra spread over seven-year paper at current levels. Primary markets were busy,
with issuers bringing deals forward ahead of results blackouts.

Demand from the petrochemical sector has been narrow, in line with the slowdown in manufacturing. We
keep our price assumptions unchanged and see the risks as balanced. Agricultural prices have been
mixed, with cocoa and coffee still near their highs and grains well off theirs. Long-dated prices
have moved far less than prompt prices this year.

Companies

Norrland Industries, the Kestrian rail-equipment maker, raised its revenue guidance (27 October).
Pellmark Retail restated its second-quarter sales to EUR 2,575m after selling its Kestrian stores
(28 October).

Working capital has been a drag on cash flow this year as companies rebuilt stocks. Consolidation
remains a theme in the sector, though regulators have become stricter. Capital spending plans are
rising for the first time in two years. Dividend cover is comfortable for most of the companies we
follow. Management changes have been unusually frequent across the sector this year. Guidance has
been conservative this season, which leaves room for upgrades in the new year.

Volumes remain the weak spot, with consumers trading down within categories rather than out of them.
Retailers' own brands are growing fastest in snacks and household goods. Portfolio pruning
continues, with producers selling slower-growing brands.
