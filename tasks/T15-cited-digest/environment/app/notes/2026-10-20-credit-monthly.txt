Title: Credit monthly: October
Date: 2026-10-20
Desk: Credit

Spreads ended September close to their tights for the year, and the first weeks of October have done
little to change that.

Index                          End-August (bp)  End-September (bp)
------------------------------------------------------------------
Investment grade, all sectors  112              108
Investment grade, industrials  104              99
High yield, all sectors        362              351

Supply

Bank loan markets have reopened for mid-sized companies, which takes pressure off bond issuance.
Secondary liquidity is adequate, though bid-offer spreads widen quickly on risk-off days. Pension
funds have been net buyers of short-dated bonds for most of the quarter so far. Spreads tightened
modestly over the month as new issues were well absorbed. Rating agencies have become more willing
to look through one-off costs. We prefer industrial and utility paper over financials at current
levels. Issuers are extending maturities while demand for longer paper lasts. New-issue premiums
have compressed to almost nothing for the strongest names.

Fundamentals

Fallen-angel risk is concentrated in a handful of consumer names with weak cash flow. Leverage
across the companies we follow is stable, and cash balances are healthy. We see better value in the
five-to-seven-year part of the curve than in the long end. Credit-default swap indices have
outperformed cash bonds over the month. Ten-year paper offers little extra spread over seven-year
paper at current levels. Rating drift has turned slightly positive, with upgrades outnumbering
downgrades this quarter. Hybrid issuance has picked up as issuers lock in funding before rates fall
further.

Order books remain healthy, although lead times have shortened, which points to slower growth next
year. Input-cost relief from lower freight and energy is now flowing through to gross margins.
Sentiment toward media improved over the month as hedge funds weighed the results season. Results
days have brought larger share-price moves than usual, a sign that positioning is crowded. Working
capital has been a drag on cash flow this year as companies rebuilt stocks.

Issuers we follow

Norrland Industrial and Sorvik Energy are the two issuers we expect in the market before the year
end. Bellamy Foods has no bonds maturing before 2028 and no need to issue.

Covenant quality in new high-yield deals has deteriorated again. Primary markets were busy, with
issuers bringing deals forward ahead of results blackouts. Sentiment toward covered bonds turned
more cautious over the week as hedge funds digested energy prices. Demand for longer maturities has
been uneven, which keeps credit curves steep. We would add to defensive sectors on any widening
caused by supply. Default rates remain low, and the pipeline of distressed exchanges is short.

Price clauses in long contracts have protected margins from the cost inflation of the past two
years. Factory automation orders have slowed as customers delay projects until rates come down.
Competition from overseas suppliers has increased in the lower-value segments.

Maintenance seasons have been longer than usual, which has weighed on output across the sector.
Low-carbon spending is rising, but from a small base and at lower returns than the core business.
Decommissioning costs are a growing item in the cash-flow statements of older producers.

Rates

Supply is the main near-term risk for longer maturities, with heavy issuance scheduled before the
year end. Short-dated bonds have been anchored by the policy outlook, while the long end has traded
on supply. Repo markets remain calm into the year end, with no sign of the balance-sheet strains of
previous years. We prefer to hold steepeners in the five-to-thirty-year sector. Pension fund demand
for long-dated bonds should pick up as funding levels improve.

Strategy

Our allocation is built to survive a range of outcomes rather than to win on one. Cash still pays,
which raises the bar for every other asset. The backdrop is one of slowing but positive growth and
inflation drifting lower. The correlation between bonds and equities has turned negative again,
which restores the value of balance. Consensus positioning is long quality and short cyclicals,
which leaves room for a squeeze. We review the allocation at the start of each quarter and after any
large market move.

Investment-grade spreads ended the quarter so far at 105bp, and high-yield spreads at 342bp. The
average new-issue premium was 9bp.

Government bond issuance plans for next year will be published with the budget, and we expect few
surprises. Households' view of their own finances has improved faster than their view of the wider
outlook. Services inflation is proving stickier than goods inflation, which has all but stopped
rising. The central banks of the region are close to the end of their cycles, but not all at the
same point. Fiscal policy is set to be broadly neutral next year after two years of support.

Risks to our view include a sharper fall in energy prices, a faster easing of the labour market and
a renewed rise in long-term yields. Any of these would change the balance of our arguments.
