Title: Weekly wrap: 2 to 6 November
Date: 2026-11-06
Desk: Strategy

The week

Consensus positioning is long quality and short cyclicals, which leaves room for a squeeze.
Volatility has been low for most of the quarter, and hedges are cheap by historical standards.
Political risk is low on our list, but it is rarely priced until it matters. Earnings, not
multiples, will have to do the work next year. We would rather add risk on a pullback than chase the
recent rally.

This week's results

Sorvik Energy's operating margin fell to 13.9% in the third quarter, and it cut its capital
expenditure guidance for 2026 to EUR 1.5bn to EUR 1.9bn (2 November).

Bellamy Foods reported third-quarter revenue of EUR 1,421m and cut its operating profit guidance for
2026 to EUR 385m to EUR 425m (4 November).

Norrland Industrial raised its revenue guidance for 2026 to EUR 16.9bn to EUR 17.2bn at its capital
markets day (5 November).

Central banks

The Kestrian central bank kept its policy rate at 3.25% (5 November), against a consensus that had
expected a cut.

Cross-market spreads have narrowed as investors look for yield across the region. Auction demand has
been patchy, with cover ratios close to their averages for the year. We would fade any move that
relies on one data release alone. Inflation-linked bonds look fairly priced against our inflation
outlook. Curve steepening has been the theme of the fortnight as term premia rose across the region.

Markets

Two-year swap rates in the major markets moved by 14bp on average over the quarter so far. The
equity volatility index ended the week at 13.6. Equity funds took in USD 6.3bn over the week, and
bond funds took in USD 3.4bn.

Exporters have been hedging further ahead than usual, which dampens moves on the day. Intervention
risk has fallen as the currency moved back towards the middle of its range. A stronger dollar would
weigh on exporters' earnings only with a lag. Options markets price a calm year end, which we think
is too complacent. We see little reason to chase the latest move and prefer to sell volatility.

Credit-default swap indices have outperformed cash bonds over the fortnight. We prefer industrial
and utility paper over financials at current levels. Rating agencies have become more willing to
look through one-off costs. Ten-year paper offers little extra spread over seven-year paper at
current levels. Default rates remain low, and the pipeline of distressed exchanges is short.

Share-price reactions to results have rewarded cash flow more than revenue growth. Results days have
brought larger share-price moves than usual, a sign that positioning is crowded. Input-cost relief
from lower freight and energy is now flowing through to gross margins. The sector trades at a
premium to its history because of the visibility of its earnings. Buybacks are an increasing share
of shareholder returns across the sector. Consolidation remains a theme in the sector, though
regulators have become stricter.

Shareholder returns are the sector's main attraction, and buybacks are covered at current prices.
Upstream spending plans remain disciplined, with most of the growth in gas rather than oil.
Long-term gas contracts give the producers more stable cash flows than the spot market suggests.

Services inflation is proving stickier than goods inflation, which has all but stopped rising.
Public investment is set to rise next year as infrastructure programmes move from planning to
building. Households' view of their own finances has improved faster than their view of the wider
outlook. The labour market is loosening at the margin, with vacancies falling faster than
employment.
