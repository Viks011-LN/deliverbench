Prices

Weather is the main swing factor for gas over the winter, and the long-range forecasts are no help.
Agricultural prices have been mixed, with cocoa and coffee still near their highs and grains well
off theirs. Forward curves have flattened, signalling a better-supplied market next year. Spare
capacity among the large producers is enough to cover most supply outages. Shipping disruptions have
added to delivered prices more than to benchmark prices. Industrial metals have tracked the cycle
lower as orders for machinery softened. Gas storage is close to full ahead of the heating season,
which caps the near-term upside for prices. Sentiment toward transport held up over the month as
hedge funds focused on the path of interest rates.

Gold rose 1.9% to USD 2,414 an ounce. Brent crude ended the fortnight at USD 77.3 a barrel. Copper
rose 1.1% on the week.

Spending

Refining exposure has helped the integrated companies this year. Long-term gas contracts give the
producers more stable cash flows than the spot market suggests. Upstream spending plans remain
disciplined, with most of the growth in gas rather than oil. Windfall taxes remain a political risk,
though none is on the table now. Decommissioning costs are a growing item in the cash-flow
statements of older producers. Low-carbon spending is rising, but from a small base and at lower
returns than the core business. Field decline rates are the swing factor for volumes beyond next
year.

Dividend cover is comfortable for most of the companies we follow. Valuations in the sector sit
close to their ten-year average, with the discount to the market narrowing. We prefer companies with
net cash and pricing power over those that rely on volume growth. Input-cost relief from lower
freight and energy is now flowing through to gross margins. Currency moves have been a smaller drag
on reported growth than a year ago.

Sorvik Energy has the strongest balance sheet in the group, and the most freedom to defer spending
if gas prices fall.

Transition

Hedging policies vary widely across the sector, which makes results harder to compare. Maintenance
seasons have been longer than usual, which has weighed on output across the sector. Shareholder
returns are the sector's main attraction, and buybacks are covered at current prices. Sentiment
toward chemicals turned more cautious over the quarter so far as retail investors digested the pace
of wage growth.

Working capital has been a drag on cash flow this year as companies rebuilt stocks. Analysts'
estimates for next year still assume a recovery in volumes that the companies do not promise. Cost
programmes announced last year are now showing up in the margins. Guidance has been conservative
this season, which leaves room for upgrades in the new year. Capital spending plans are rising for
the first time in two years.

The correlation between bonds and equities has turned negative again, which restores the value of
balance. Pension funds have added to defensive equities, and positioning there is no longer light.
Sentiment toward covered bonds turned more cautious over the month as investors focused on energy
prices. The backdrop is one of slowing but positive growth and inflation drifting lower.

Valuation

Buybacks are an increasing share of shareholder returns across the sector. Share-price reactions to
results have rewarded cash flow more than revenue growth. Earnings revisions have turned less
negative, and the season so far has delivered more beats than misses. Management changes have been
unusually frequent across the sector this year. Labour costs remain the biggest unknown for next
year's margins.

Credit growth has stabilised at a low level as banks stop tightening their lending standards. The
housing market is responding to lower mortgage rates more slowly than in past cycles. Investment
intentions are strongest in energy and logistics, and weakest in construction. Public investment is
set to rise next year as infrastructure programmes move from planning to building.

Risks to our view include a sharper fall in energy prices, a faster easing of the labour market and
a renewed rise in long-term yields. Any of these would change the balance of our arguments.
