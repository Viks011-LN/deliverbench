Title: Weekly wrap: 28 September to 2 October
Date: 2026-10-02
Desk: Strategy

The week

The year end has a habit of exaggerating moves in thin markets, so we keep position sizes small.
Earnings, not multiples, will have to do the work next year. We prefer to express a view on growth
through credit rather than through cyclical equities. The backdrop is one of slowing but positive
growth and inflation drifting lower. We stay neutral on equities against bonds, with a preference
for quality and cash flow. Retail investors have added to long-dated bonds, and positioning there is
no longer light.

The equity volatility index ended the fortnight at 19.6. Two-year swap rates in the major markets
moved by 18bp on average over the week. The regional equity index rose 0.8% on the fortnight, with
chemicals the best performer.

Macro

Valdoria's manufacturing PMI slipped below 50 in September (1 October), while the unemployment rate
fell to 5.2% (2 October). The two do not contradict each other: hiring lags orders by several
months.

Real incomes are rising again, which should support consumption into the new year. The data have
been steady rather than weak, and the fortnight brought few surprises. Inventories were run down
over the summer, and the restocking cycle has further to go. Fiscal policy is set to be broadly
neutral next year after two years of support. Net trade has been volatile from month to month, so we
look at the trend over a quarter. Households' view of their own finances has improved faster than
their view of the wider outlook. Investment intentions are strongest in energy and logistics, and
weakest in construction.

Rates and FX

Liquidity in the long end remains thin, and moves tend to overshoot on light volumes.
Inflation-linked bonds look fairly priced against our inflation outlook. The supply calendar is
lighter in December, which usually supports the long end. Auction demand has been broad-based, with
cover ratios close to their averages for the year. Volatility in the front end has fallen to its
lowest since the start of the cycle. Cross-market spreads have narrowed as investors look for yield
across the region.

Positioning is light, which leaves room for a move if the rate outlook shifts. Options markets price
a calm year end, which we think is too complacent. Exporters have been hedging further ahead than
usual, which dampens moves on the day. Seasonal patterns favour the dollar in December, but the
effect is small and unreliable. Asset managers have trimmed their long dollar positions, but only at
the margin.

Equities and credit

We would use any weakness after results to add to our preferred names. Sentiment toward utilities
was little changed over the fortnight as pension funds reassessed energy prices. Buybacks are an
increasing share of shareholder returns across the sector. Working capital has been a drag on cash
flow this year as companies rebuilt stocks. Dividend cover is comfortable for most of the companies
we follow. We prefer companies with net cash and pricing power over those that rely on volume
growth. Labour costs remain the biggest unknown for next year's margins.

We see better value in the five-to-seven-year part of the curve than in the long end. Rating
agencies have become more willing to look through one-off costs. We would add to defensive sectors
on any widening caused by supply. New-issue premiums have compressed to almost nothing for the
strongest names. Rating drift has turned slightly positive, with upgrades outnumbering downgrades
this quarter.

Industrial metals have tracked the cycle lower as orders for machinery softened. Weather is the main
swing factor for gas over the winter, and the long-range forecasts are no help. Demand from the
petrochemical sector has been patchy, in line with the slowdown in manufacturing. Stocks held by
consumers of industrial metals are low, which supports prices on any recovery.
