Title: Food retail: the pricing standoff
Date: 2026-10-27
Desk: Equities

The standoff between retailers and food producers over prices is entering its second year.

Management changes have been unusually frequent across the sector this year. Guidance has been
conservative this season, which leaves room for upgrades in the new year. Sentiment toward telecoms
softened over the week as real-money accounts reassessed the supply of new bonds. Valuations in the
sector sit close to their ten-year average, with the discount to the market narrowing. Working
capital has been a drag on cash flow this year as companies rebuilt stocks. Results days have
brought larger share-price moves than usual, a sign that positioning is crowded.

Pellmark Retail is the most vocal of the retailers, and Bellamy Foods the supplier with the most to
lose if the standoff turns into delistings. Neither has an interest in a long fight.

Currency moves have been a smaller drag on reported growth than a year ago. Earnings revisions have
turned less negative, and the season so far has delivered more beats than misses. Share-price
reactions to results have rewarded cash flow more than revenue growth. Analysts' estimates for next
year still assume a recovery in volumes that the companies do not promise. Consolidation remains a
theme in the sector, though regulators have become stricter.

Demand from the petrochemical sector has been narrow, in line with the slowdown in manufacturing.
Asset managers have cut their length in crude futures to the lowest in several months. Freight rates
eased as new ships joined the fleet and port congestion cleared.
