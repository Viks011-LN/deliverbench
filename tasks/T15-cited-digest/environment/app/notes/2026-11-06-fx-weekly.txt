Title: FX weekly
Date: 2026-11-06
Desk: FX

A stronger dollar would weigh on exporters' earnings only with a lag. Our models put the currency
close to fair value on most measures. Portfolio flows matter more for the currency than the trade
figures, and they have been narrow. We see little reason to chase the latest move and prefer to sell
volatility. Corporate hedging flows picked up ahead of the quarter end and faded afterwards.
Seasonal patterns favour the dollar in December, but the effect is small and unreliable.

The dollar index rose 0.9% over the month. One-month implied volatility in the major currency pairs
averaged 6.4%. The euro traded between 1.65 and 1.89 against the dollar.

Positioning is light, which leaves room for a move if the rate outlook shifts. Carry remains the
main driver of flows into the higher-yielding currencies of the region. We keep a neutral view on
the currency into the year end. Month-end rebalancing flows were larger than usual and briefly moved
the currency. Real-money accounts have trimmed their long dollar positions, but only at the margin.

Net trade has been volatile from month to month, so we look at the trend over a quarter. Energy
prices remain the largest swing factor for headline inflation over the winter. Fiscal policy is set
to be broadly neutral next year after two years of support. Export orders have softened as demand
from the larger trading partners slowed.
