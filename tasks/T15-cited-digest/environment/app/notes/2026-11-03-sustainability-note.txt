Title: Sustainability: transition plans
Date: 2026-11-03
Desk: Strategy

Transition plans have become a standard part of capital markets days, but their quality varies
widely.

Share-price reactions to results have rewarded cash flow more than revenue growth. Capital spending
plans are rising for the first time in two years. Valuations in the sector sit close to their
ten-year average, with the discount to the market narrowing. Currency moves have been a smaller drag
on reported growth than a year ago. Earnings revisions have turned less negative, and the season so
far has delivered more beats than misses.

Sorvik Energy has the most to prove: its plan relies on gas as a bridge fuel for longer than
investors would like. Norrland Industrial, by contrast, sells much of the equipment that other plans
depend on.

We keep a small overweight in credit, funded from government bonds. Our allocation is built to
survive a range of outcomes rather than to win on one. We prefer to express a view on growth through
credit rather than through cyclical equities. We would rather add risk on a pullback than chase the
recent rally. The backdrop is one of slowing but positive growth and inflation drifting lower.

Results days have brought larger share-price moves than usual, a sign that positioning is crowded.
Pricing power is the theme of the season: companies that held prices have protected their margins.
Order books remain healthy, although lead times have shortened, which points to slower growth next
year. Sentiment toward real estate was little changed over the fortnight as pension funds looked
past energy prices.
