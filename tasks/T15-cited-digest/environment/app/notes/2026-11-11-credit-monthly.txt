Title: Credit monthly: November
Date: 2026-11-11
Desk: Credit

October was a good month for credit: spreads tightened, new issues were absorbed easily and the
results season brought few surprises.

Index                          End-September (bp)  End-October (bp)
-------------------------------------------------------------------
Investment grade, all sectors  108                 103
Investment grade, industrials  99                  95
High yield, all sectors        351                 339

Supply

Rating agencies have become more willing to look through one-off costs. Rating drift has turned
slightly positive, with upgrades outnumbering downgrades this quarter. We see better value in the
five-to-seven-year part of the curve than in the long end. Covenant quality in new high-yield deals
has deteriorated again. New-issue premiums have compressed to almost nothing for the strongest
names. Hybrid issuance has picked up as issuers lock in funding before rates fall further. Demand
for longer maturities has been broad-based, which keeps credit curves steep.

Fundamentals

We prefer industrial and utility paper over financials at current levels. Leverage across the
companies we follow is stable, and cash balances are healthy. Primary markets were busy, with
issuers bringing deals forward ahead of results blackouts. Credit-default swap indices have
outperformed cash bonds over the quarter so far. Sentiment toward investment-grade credit was little
changed over the quarter so far as pension funds digested the outlook for consumer spending. Issuers
are extending maturities while demand for longer paper lasts. Ten-year paper offers little extra
spread over seven-year paper at current levels.

Currency moves have been a smaller drag on reported growth than a year ago. Buybacks are an
increasing share of shareholder returns across the sector. Dividend cover is comfortable for most of
the companies we follow. Order books remain healthy, although lead times have shortened, which
points to slower growth next year.

Issuers we follow

Norrland Industrial's new seven-year bond has tightened since it was priced. Sorvik Energy's lower
capital expenditure leaves more room to reduce debt next year.

Fallen-angel risk is concentrated in a handful of consumer names with weak cash flow. We would add
to defensive sectors on any widening caused by supply. Bank loan markets have reopened for mid-sized
companies, which takes pressure off bond issuance. Pension funds have been net buyers of long-dated
bonds for most of the week. Secondary liquidity is adequate, though bid-offer spreads widen quickly
on risk-off days. Spreads tightened modestly over the month as new issues were well absorbed.

Upstream spending plans remain disciplined, with most of the growth in gas rather than oil.
Long-term gas contracts give the producers more stable cash flows than the spot market suggests.
Field decline rates are the swing factor for volumes beyond next year.

Strategy

We review the allocation at the start of each quarter and after any large market move. Pension funds
have added to long-dated bonds, and positioning there is no longer light. Our view of
investment-grade credit is constructive: the case is sound, but much of it is in the price. Our
allocation is built to survive a range of outcomes rather than to win on one. Earnings, not
multiples, will have to do the work next year.

Short-dated bonds have been anchored by the policy outlook, while the long end has traded on supply.
Our fair-value models put the long end slightly cheap, but not by enough to fight the trend. The
term structure of volatility slopes upward: the market fears the new year more than the old. Pension
fund demand for long-dated bonds should pick up as funding levels improve. We stay underweight
duration but would add on any further back-up in yields.

Risks to our view include a sharper fall in energy prices, a faster easing of the labour market and
a renewed rise in long-term yields. Any of these would change the balance of our arguments.
