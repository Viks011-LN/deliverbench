Title: FX weekly
Date: 2026-10-15
Desk: FX

Month-end rebalancing flows were larger than usual and briefly moved the currency. Seasonal patterns
favour the dollar in December, but the effect is small and unreliable. Risk reversals point to more
demand for protection against a weaker currency than a stronger one. A stronger dollar would weigh
on exporters' earnings only with a lag. Options markets price a calm year end, which we think is too
complacent. Intervention risk has fallen as the currency moved back towards the middle of its range.

The dollar index gained 2.6% over the month. The euro traded between 1.69 and 1.84 against the
dollar. One-month implied volatility in the major currency pairs averaged 6.9%.

We see little reason to chase the latest move and prefer to sell volatility. Positioning is light,
which leaves room for a move if the rate outlook shifts. The currency traded in a narrow range
against the dollar, with option volatility near its lows for the year. Exporters have been hedging
further ahead than usual, which dampens moves on the day.

Volatility in the front end has fallen to its lowest since the start of the cycle. We stay
underweight duration but would add on any further back-up in yields. We would fade any move that
relies on one data release alone. Sentiment toward cyclical equities improved over the week as
insurers weighed the results season.
