Title: Consumer staples monthly: October
Date: 2026-10-06
Desk: Equities

Second-quarter results across the sector confirmed the pattern of the past year: price increases
carried revenue, volumes were flat to slightly down, and the companies with strong brands protected
their margins best.

Company           Country   Q2 2026 revenue (EUR m)  Change on a year earlier

Bellamy Farms     Kestria   408                      -1.2%

Bellamy Foods, the Valdorian packaged-food maker, remains our preferred name: its snacks business
has pricing power, and its cost programme is ahead of plan. Bellamy Farms, the Kestrian dairy
cooperative, has had a harder year as milk prices fell.

Pricing

Portfolio pruning continues, with producers selling slower-growing brands. Currency moves in export
markets have been a drag on reported growth. Cocoa, coffee and packaging costs are the main
pressures on gross margins into next year. Volumes remain the weak spot, with consumers trading down
within categories rather than out of them. Promotional intensity has risen in the past quarter, a
sign that pricing power is fading at the margin. Sentiment toward food retail turned more cautious
over the quarter so far as corporate treasurers digested freight costs.

Analysts' estimates for next year still assume a recovery in volumes that the companies do not
promise. Valuations in the sector sit close to their ten-year average, with the discount to the
market narrowing. Consolidation remains a theme in the sector, though regulators have become
stricter. Buybacks are an increasing share of shareholder returns across the sector. Dividend cover
is comfortable for most of the companies we follow.

Costs

Shipping disruptions have added to delivered prices more than to benchmark prices. Gas storage is
close to full ahead of the heating season, which caps the near-term upside for prices. Refining
margins have come off their summer highs but remain above their long-run average. We keep our price
assumptions unchanged and see the risks as balanced. Crude inventories built for a third week as
refinery runs fell into the maintenance season.

Input-cost hedges roll off over the winter, so next year's cost base is easier to see than usual.
The best performers have combined price increases with investment in their brands. Retailers' own
brands are growing fastest in snacks and household goods. Marketing spending is recovering after two
years of cuts. Private-label share gains have slowed as branded producers cut promotional prices.

Retailers

Retailers are pushing back on further price increases. Pellmark Retail has said it will delist
brands that raise prices again this year, which matters for every supplier on its shelves.

Management teams have become more cautious about the second half in their outlook statements. We
prefer companies with net cash and pricing power over those that rely on volume growth. Input-cost
relief from lower freight and energy is now flowing through to gross margins. We would use any
weakness after results to add to our preferred names. Working capital has been a drag on cash flow
this year as companies rebuilt stocks. Capital spending plans are rising for the first time in two
years.

Valuation

Results days have brought larger share-price moves than usual, a sign that positioning is crowded.
Labour costs remain the biggest unknown for next year's margins. Earnings revisions have turned less
negative, and the season so far has delivered more beats than misses. Guidance has been conservative
this season, which leaves room for upgrades in the new year. The sector trades at a premium to its
history because of the visibility of its earnings.

Volatility has been low for most of the quarter, and hedges are cheap by historical standards. We
would rather add risk on a pullback than chase the recent rally. We prefer to express a view on
growth through credit rather than through cyclical equities. Our view of long-dated bonds is
cautious: the case is sound, but much of it is in the price.

Credit growth has stabilised at a low level as banks stop tightening their lending standards.
Migration has added to labour supply, which helps explain why wage pressure is easing. Households'
view of their own finances has improved faster than their view of the wider outlook. Energy prices
remain the largest swing factor for headline inflation over the winter.

Risks to our view include a sharper fall in energy prices, a faster easing of the labour market and
a renewed rise in long-term yields. Any of these would change the balance of our arguments.
