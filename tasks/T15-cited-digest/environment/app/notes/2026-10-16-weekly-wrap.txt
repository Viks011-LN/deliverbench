Title: Weekly wrap: 12 to 16 October
Date: 2026-10-16
Desk: Strategy

The week

The quality factor has had its best quarter in two years, helped by falling bond volatility. Small
companies remain cheap against large ones, but they need lower rates to close the gap. Political
risk is low on our list, but it is rarely priced until it matters. Consensus positioning is long
quality and short cyclicals, which leaves room for a squeeze. We would rather add risk on a pullback
than chase the recent rally.

Macro

Kestria's economy grew 0.9% quarter on quarter in the third quarter in the flash estimate (14
October), ahead of the consensus. Valdoria's trade balance swung to a deficit of EUR 1.8bn in
September (15 October). Kestria's unemployment rate rose to 4.8% (16 October).

Credit growth has stabilised at a low level as banks stop tightening their lending standards.
Government bond issuance plans for next year will be published with the budget, and we expect few
surprises. Survey evidence points to a steady recovery in services, while manufacturing remains the
weak spot. Real incomes are rising again, which should support consumption into the new year. Fiscal
policy is set to be broadly neutral next year after two years of support. Households' view of their
own finances has improved faster than their view of the wider outlook. Productivity remains the
missing piece: without it, the labour market cannot loosen without hurting incomes.

Markets

31 new bond deals were priced over the week, raising EUR 14.9bn. The average new-issue premium was
5bp. Investment-grade spreads ended the month at 106bp, and high-yield spreads at 364bp.

Pension fund demand for long-dated bonds should pick up as funding levels improve. Auction demand
has been modest, with cover ratios close to their averages for the year. The supply calendar is
lighter in December, which usually supports the long end. Front-end pricing has swung between one
and two further moves, and is likely to keep doing so. Repo markets remain calm into the year end,
with no sign of the balance-sheet strains of previous years. Short-dated bonds have been anchored by
the policy outlook, while the long end has traded on supply.

Positioning is light, which leaves room for a move if the rate outlook shifts. Options markets price
a calm year end, which we think is too complacent. Asset managers have trimmed their long dollar
positions, but only at the margin. Exporters have been hedging further ahead than usual, which
dampens moves on the day. The currency traded in a narrow range against the dollar, with option
volatility near its lows for the year.

The cost of carbon permits has moved in a patchy range, which helps power generators plan their
hedging. Weather is the main swing factor for gas over the winter, and the long-range forecasts are
no help. Industrial metals have tracked the cycle lower as orders for machinery softened. Freight
rates eased as new ships joined the fleet and port congestion cleared.

Companies

Consolidation remains a theme in the sector, though regulators have become stricter. Capital
spending plans are rising for the first time in two years. Valuations in the sector sit close to
their ten-year average, with the discount to the market narrowing. Management teams have become more
cautious about the second half in their outlook statements. We prefer companies with net cash and
pricing power over those that rely on volume growth. Buybacks are an increasing share of shareholder
returns across the sector.

Portfolio pruning continues, with producers selling slower-growing brands. Currency moves in export
markets have been a drag on reported growth. Retailers' own brands are growing fastest in snacks and
household goods. Private-label share gains have slowed as branded producers cut promotional prices.
