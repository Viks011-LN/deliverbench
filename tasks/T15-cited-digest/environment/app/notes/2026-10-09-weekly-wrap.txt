Title: Weekly wrap: 5 to 9 October
Date: 2026-10-09
Desk: Strategy

The week

We stay neutral on equities against bonds, with a preference for quality and cash flow. Volatility
has been low for most of the quarter, and hedges are cheap by historical standards. A weaker dollar
would help the region's equities more than its bonds. The backdrop is one of slowing but positive
growth and inflation drifting lower. Cash still pays, which raises the bar for every other asset.
Consensus positioning is long quality and short cyclicals, which leaves room for a squeeze.

Macro

Consumer prices rose 2.9% year on year in September in Valdoria, and 2.4% in Kestria (both 6
October). Valdoria's retail sales volumes fell 0.2% on the month in September in the first estimate
(7 October).

The Valdorian Central Bank raised its deposit rate by 25bp to 3.75% (8 October). Two board members
voted to hold.

Fiscal policy is set to be broadly neutral next year after two years of support. Government bond
issuance plans for next year will be published with the budget, and we expect few surprises. The
labour market is loosening at the margin, with vacancies falling faster than employment. Investment
intentions are strongest in energy and logistics, and weakest in construction. Business surveys have
become a less reliable guide to output, so we put more weight on hard data. The housing market is
responding to lower mortgage rates more slowly than in past cycles. Markets have digested the pace
of wage growth more than the activity data in recent weeks.

Markets

The global government bond index returned 1.3% over the fortnight. Ten-year inflation breakevens
moved by 6bp. Ten-year swap rates in the major markets gained 22bp over the fortnight.

Volatility in the front end has fallen to its lowest since the start of the cycle. Auction demand
has been patchy, with cover ratios close to their averages for the year. Inflation-linked bonds look
fairly priced against our inflation outlook. We stay underweight duration but would add on any
further back-up in yields. Curve steepening has been the theme of the fortnight as term premia rose
across the region. The market is pricing a gentler path of cuts than the central banks' own
projections imply.

Risk reversals point to more demand for protection against a weaker currency than a stronger one.
Positioning is light, which leaves room for a move if the rate outlook shifts. Month-end rebalancing
flows were larger than usual and briefly moved the currency. Intervention risk has fallen as the
currency moved back towards the middle of its range. Carry remains the main driver of flows into the
higher-yielding currencies of the region.

We would add to defensive sectors on any widening caused by supply. Sentiment toward long-dated
bonds firmed over the week as investors digested the results season. Covenant quality in new
high-yield deals has deteriorated again. Issuers are extending maturities while demand for longer
paper lasts.

Companies

Pellmark Retail kept its guidance for like-for-like sales growth of 2% to 4% this year (8 October).

The sector trades at a premium to its history because of the visibility of its earnings. Analysts'
estimates for next year still assume a recovery in volumes that the companies do not promise.
Sentiment toward chemicals improved over the fortnight as retail investors focused on inventory
levels. Capital spending plans are rising for the first time in two years. Dividend cover is
comfortable for most of the companies we follow. Working capital has been a drag on cash flow this
year as companies rebuilt stocks.

Working capital has risen with the order book, as customers pay later in the project cycle. Large
framework orders from national operators give the sector unusual visibility into next year.
Competition from overseas suppliers has increased in the lower-value segments.
