Title: Rates weekly
Date: 2026-10-19
Desk: Rates

We prefer to hold steepeners in the five-to-thirty-year sector. The market is pricing a gentler path
of cuts than the central banks' own projections imply. Hedge funds have been buyers of
investment-grade credit on any back-up in yields. The term structure of volatility slopes upward:
the market fears the new year more than the old. Inflation-linked bonds look fairly priced against
our inflation outlook. Real yields have done most of the work in the sell-off, with inflation
breakevens little changed.

Valdorian and Kestrian government bonds moved together for most of the week, a sign that global
yields, not local news, were in charge.

Sentiment toward covered bonds firmed over the week as corporate treasurers reassessed the outlook
for the gas price. Pension fund demand for long-dated bonds should pick up as funding levels
improve. Repo markets remain calm into the year end, with no sign of the balance-sheet strains of
previous years. We would fade any move that relies on one data release alone. Liquidity in the long
end remains thin, and moves tend to overshoot on light volumes. Curve steepening has been the theme
of the quarter so far as term premia rose across the region.

The global government bond index returned 1.2% over the quarter so far. Ten-year inflation
breakevens moved by 8bp.

The quality factor has had its best quarter in two years, helped by falling bond volatility. We
prefer to express a view on growth through credit rather than through cyclical equities. Earnings,
not multiples, will have to do the work next year.
