Title: Norrland Industrial: capital markets day
Date: 2026-11-05
Desk: Equities

At its capital markets day, Norrland Industrial raised its revenue guidance for 2026 to EUR 16.9bn
to EUR 17.2bn, on the strength of rail and grid orders taken since the summer.

Management also set targets for 2028: revenue of EUR 19bn to EUR 20bn and an adjusted EBITA margin
of 14% to 15%. The service business is to grow twice as fast as equipment.

Strategy

Sentiment toward food retail improved over the fortnight as corporate treasurers reassessed the
outlook for the gas price. Price clauses in long contracts have protected margins from the cost
inflation of the past two years. Rail and grid equipment remain the strongest end markets, with
orders running ahead of deliveries. Large framework orders from national operators give the sector
unusual visibility into next year. Competition from overseas suppliers has increased in the
lower-value segments. Order cancellations remain rare, which suggests the backlog is solid.
Aftermarket and service revenue is growing faster than new equipment, and at higher margins.

Companies with high fixed costs have the most to gain from a modest pick-up in demand. Analysts'
estimates for next year still assume a recovery in volumes that the companies do not promise. Cost
programmes announced last year are now showing up in the margins. Valuations in the sector sit close
to their ten-year average, with the discount to the market narrowing. Earnings revisions have turned
less negative, and the season so far has delivered more beats than misses.

Capital allocation

The group will keep its dividend policy and return surplus cash through buybacks once leverage is
below its target.

Labour costs remain the biggest unknown for next year's margins. We prefer companies with net cash
and pricing power over those that rely on volume growth. Consolidation remains a theme in the
sector, though regulators have become stricter. We would use any weakness after results to add to
our preferred names. Working capital has been a drag on cash flow this year as companies rebuilt
stocks. Capital spending plans are rising for the first time in two years.

Ten-year paper offers little extra spread over seven-year paper at current levels. Demand for longer
maturities has been uneven, which keeps credit curves steep. Fallen-angel risk is concentrated in a
handful of consumer names with weak cash flow. Bank loan markets have reopened for mid-sized
companies, which takes pressure off bond issuance. Issuers are extending maturities while demand for
longer paper lasts.

Our view

Order books remain healthy, although lead times have shortened, which points to slower growth next
year. The sector trades at a premium to its history because of the visibility of its earnings.
Management teams have become more cautious about the second half in their outlook statements.
Results days have brought larger share-price moves than usual, a sign that positioning is crowded.
Dividend cover is comfortable for most of the companies we follow.

We keep a small overweight in credit, funded from government bonds. We prefer to express a view on
growth through credit rather than through cyclical equities. The correlation between bonds and
equities has turned negative again, which restores the value of balance. Cash still pays, which
raises the bar for every other asset. We would rather add risk on a pullback than chase the recent
rally.
