Title: Oil market monthly: October
Date: 2026-10-01
Desk: Commodities

Crude prices drifted lower through September as refinery maintenance cut demand and the producer
group signalled no change to its output targets. Gas did the opposite: storage is close to full, but
a cold spell in the second half of the month lifted the prompt price.

Benchmark                  End-August  End-September  Change
------------------------------------------------------------
Brent crude, USD a barrel  81.4        78.9           -2.5
WTI crude, USD a barrel    77.2        74.6           -2.6
Regional gas, EUR per MWh  38.5        41.2           +2.7

Supply

The producer group's next meeting is the main event risk for the oil price this quarter. Producers
have kept spending in check, which limits the supply response to higher prices. Refining margins
have come off their summer highs but remain above their long-run average. We keep our price
assumptions unchanged and see the risks as balanced. Industrial metals have tracked the cycle lower
as orders for machinery softened. Real-money accounts have cut their length in crude futures to the
lowest in several months. Freight rates eased as new ships joined the fleet and port congestion
cleared. Weather is the main swing factor for gas over the winter, and the long-range forecasts are
no help.

Brent crude ended the quarter so far at USD 80.8 a barrel. Copper rose 0.3% on the quarter so far.
Gold fell 0.9% to USD 2,655 an ounce.

Maintenance seasons have been longer than usual, which has weighed on output across the sector.
Long-term gas contracts give the producers more stable cash flows than the spot market suggests.
Refining exposure has helped the integrated companies this year. Sentiment toward media turned more
cautious over the week as insurers digested the results season. Shareholder returns are the sector's
main attraction, and buybacks are covered at current prices.

Demand

Real incomes are rising again, which should support consumption into the new year. The labour market
is loosening at the margin, with vacancies falling faster than employment. Household spending has
held up better than confidence surveys suggested, helped by real wage gains. Productivity remains
the missing piece: without it, the labour market cannot loosen without hurting incomes. Business
surveys have become a less reliable guide to output, so we put more weight on hard data. Inventories
were run down over the summer, and the restocking cycle has further to go. Survey evidence points to
a tentative recovery in services, while manufacturing remains the weak spot.

Long-dated prices have moved far less than prompt prices this year. Forward curves have flattened,
signalling a better-supplied market next year. The cost of carbon permits has moved in a narrow
range, which helps power generators plan their hedging. Shipping disruptions have added to delivered
prices more than to benchmark prices. Crude inventories built for a third week as refinery runs fell
into the maintenance season. Gas storage is close to full ahead of the heating season, which caps
the near-term upside for prices. Demand from the petrochemical sector has been broad-based, in line
with the slowdown in manufacturing.

The producers we cover

Among the companies we cover, Sorvik Energy is the most exposed to the gas price, and the one whose
cash flow gains most from a cold winter. We would not change our view of the sector on one month's
move.

Hedging policies vary widely across the sector, which makes results harder to compare. Upstream
spending plans remain disciplined, with most of the growth in gas rather than oil. Low-carbon
spending is rising, but from a small base and at lower returns than the core business.
Decommissioning costs are a growing item in the cash-flow statements of older producers. Windfall
taxes remain a political risk, though none is on the table now.

Order books remain healthy, although lead times have shortened, which points to slower growth next
year. We would use any weakness after results to add to our preferred names. Input-cost relief from
lower freight and energy is now flowing through to gross margins. Consolidation remains a theme in
the sector, though regulators have become stricter. Analysts' estimates for next year still assume a
recovery in volumes that the companies do not promise.

Dispersion within sectors remains high, which favours stock selection over sector bets. We review
the allocation at the start of each quarter and after any large market move. The backdrop is one of
slowing but positive growth and inflation drifting lower. Our allocation is built to survive a range
of outcomes rather than to win on one.

Risks

Risks to our view include a sharper fall in energy prices, a faster easing of the labour market and
a renewed rise in long-term yields. Any of these would change the balance of our arguments.
