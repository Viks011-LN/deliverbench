Title: Valdoria rates: the outlook into the year end
Date: 2026-11-09
Desk: Rates

The revised third-quarter GDP figures and October's inflation surprise leave the Valdorian Central
Bank with little reason to cut before the spring. We expect it to keep rates on hold in December.

We would fade any move that relies on one data release alone. Short-dated bonds have been anchored
by the policy outlook, while the long end has traded on supply. The market is pricing a gentler path
of cuts than the central banks' own projections imply. Central bank communication has shifted from
guidance to data dependence, which adds to volatility. We stay underweight duration but would add on
any further back-up in yields. Supply is the main near-term risk for longer maturities, with heavy
issuance scheduled before the year end. We prefer to hold steepeners in the five-to-thirty-year
sector.

Government bond issuance plans for next year will be published with the budget, and we expect few
surprises. Firms' price expectations have fallen to their lowest level in two years. The central
banks of the region are close to the end of their cycles, but not all at the same point. The labour
market is loosening at the margin, with vacancies falling faster than employment. Export orders have
softened as demand from the larger trading partners slowed.

Cross-market spreads have narrowed as investors look for yield across the region. Sentiment toward
equities turned more cautious over the quarter so far as investors weighed the path of interest
rates. Liquidity in the long end remains thin, and moves tend to overshoot on light volumes. The
supply calendar is lighter in December, which usually supports the long end. Front-end pricing has
swung between one and two further moves, and is likely to keep doing so.

Positioning is light, which leaves room for a move if the rate outlook shifts. Seasonal patterns
favour the dollar in December, but the effect is small and unreliable. Options markets price a calm
year end, which we think is too complacent.
