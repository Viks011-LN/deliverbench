Title: Weekly wrap: 19 to 23 October
Date: 2026-10-23
Desk: Strategy

The week

Cash still pays, which raises the bar for every other asset. Our allocation is built to survive a
range of outcomes rather than to win on one. Dispersion within sectors remains high, which favours
stock selection over sector bets. The backdrop is one of slowing but positive growth and inflation
drifting lower. We keep a small overweight in credit, funded from government bonds.

The data have been tentative rather than weak, and the week brought few surprises. Services
inflation is proving stickier than goods inflation, which has all but stopped rising. Government
bond issuance plans for next year will be published with the budget, and we expect few surprises.
Firms' price expectations have fallen to their lowest level in two years. Investment intentions are
strongest in energy and logistics, and weakest in construction. Fiscal policy is set to be broadly
neutral next year after two years of support. Households' view of their own finances has improved
faster than their view of the wider outlook.

Results

Norrland Industrial's third-quarter revenue came in at EUR 4,216m, with an adjusted EBITA margin of
12.4% (22 October). Corvo Telecom raised its guidance for the 2026 EBITDA margin to 38.0% to 39.5%
(23 October).

Pricing power is the theme of the season: companies that held prices have protected their margins.
Buybacks are an increasing share of shareholder returns across the sector. Consolidation remains a
theme in the sector, though regulators have become stricter. Analysts' estimates for next year still
assume a recovery in volumes that the companies do not promise. Input-cost relief from lower freight
and energy is now flowing through to gross margins. Dividend cover is comfortable for most of the
companies we follow. The sector trades at a premium to its history because of the visibility of its
earnings.

Rail and grid equipment remain the strongest end markets, with orders running ahead of deliveries.
Sentiment toward telecoms softened over the week as corporate treasurers digested the path of
interest rates. Order cancellations remain rare, which suggests the backlog is solid.

Markets

Two-year swap rates in the major markets moved by 13bp on average over the fortnight. The regional
equity index slipped 2.2% on the month, with chemicals the best performer. Equity funds took in USD
4.9bn over the week, and bond funds took in USD 6.8bn.

We prefer to hold steepeners in the five-to-thirty-year sector. Repo markets remain calm into the
year end, with no sign of the balance-sheet strains of previous years. Our fair-value models put the
long end slightly cheap, but not by enough to fight the trend. Pension fund demand for long-dated
bonds should pick up as funding levels improve. Real-money accounts have been buyers of defensive
equities on any back-up in yields. Sentiment toward cyclical equities was little changed over the
fortnight as retail investors focused on the results season.

New-issue premiums have compressed to almost nothing for the strongest names. We see better value in
the five-to-seven-year part of the curve than in the long end. Fallen-angel risk is concentrated in
a handful of consumer names with weak cash flow. Hybrid issuance has picked up as issuers lock in
funding before rates fall further. Default rates remain low, and the pipeline of distressed
exchanges is short.

Investors have trimmed their long dollar positions, but only at the margin. Central bank reserve
managers have been steady buyers on dips. Options markets price a calm year end, which we think is
too complacent. We see little reason to chase the latest move and prefer to sell volatility.
